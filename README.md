# Pip's World 🐶🗺️

A free, ad-free app for children aged 4 to 6. Children pick a place on Pip's map and play there with Pip the pup. Four worlds are open: the Rescue Station (the original *To the Rescue!* app), the Zoo, Monster Maths and School. Three more are on the way: Supermarket, Toy Shop and New Puppies.

At the Rescue Station, Pip shows children 9 rescue vehicles, each with a short mission to play. There are also 12 games, a sticker book, team badges and a Rescue Cadet certificate. Everything is read aloud, and the app works fully offline once it is installed.

## 📲 Install it on an Android tablet

1. On the tablet, open the link above in **Chrome**.
2. Tap the red play button once. This lets the app finish saving itself for offline use, which takes a few seconds.
3. Install it in either of these ways:
   - tap the yellow **📲 Install app** button that pops up in the bottom-left corner a few seconds after the page opens, or
   - open the **⋮** menu and choose **Add to Home screen** or **Install app**.
4. Open the app from its new icon on the home screen. It runs full screen and sideways, and needs no internet from then on.

The grown-ups area also has an **Install app** button when the tablet supports it. To open the grown-ups area, press and hold the bottom-right button for 3 seconds.

> **Narrator voice offline:** the narrator uses the tablet's own text-to-speech voice. To make sure it talks without the internet, go to **Settings → Accessibility (or System) → Text-to-speech**, choose **Speech Services by Google**, and download the **English (United Kingdom)** voice.

On iPad, open the link in Safari, tap **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**.

### If Chrome says "already installed" or "Could not open app"

This means the tablet still remembers an earlier install that didn't finish properly. To clear it:

1. Remove any old **To the Rescue** icon: long-press it, then choose **Uninstall** (or **Remove**).
2. In Chrome, open **⋮ → Settings → Site settings → All sites** and find **richardbrb9-coder.github.io**. Tap it, then tap **Clear & reset**.
   - This also clears saved progress in any other apps you use from richardbrb9-coder.github.io.
3. Restart the tablet.
4. Open the link above in Chrome. Wait for the page to load fully, then tap play once.
5. Tap the **📲 Install app** button, or choose **⋮ → Install app** (or **Add to home screen → Install**). Wait for Chrome's "added to home screen" message before opening the app.

If Chrome doesn't offer to install, a white **📲 How to install** button shows these steps on screen instead.

### Other ways to install

- **Another browser:** open the link in **Microsoft Edge** or **Samsung Internet** and use its **Add to Home screen** option. Each browser keeps its own install records, so this sidesteps Chrome's stuck one.
- **An APK file:** go to [pwabuilder.com](https://www.pwabuilder.com), paste the "Play it now" link, choose **Package for stores → Android**, and download the zip. Upload the `.apk` file inside it to Google Drive, open it on the tablet, and allow **Install unknown apps** when Android asks. Keep the signing key file from the zip if you want to update the app later.

  - Meet six monsters, each made of a shape and a number to count: Gobble (a circle with one eye), Zug (a square with two horns), Trixie (a triangle with three eyes), Boxy (a rectangle on four legs), Twinkle (a star with five points) and Hexie (a hexagon with six sides).   - First Day: a 7-page picture story, read aloud, about Pip's first day at school, from feeling wobbly at the gate to making friends and going down the slide.

  - School Train: the syllable train with 12 school words (pencil, teacher, scissors, playground, crayon, lunchbox, computer, alphabet, xylophone, uniform, watercolour, macaroni).
  - Pack My Bag: drag the things we need for school into the school bag, and leave the rest at home (5, then 6, then 8 things).

## For developers

The app is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no build step. All artwork is drawn in code as SVG, and the sound effects are made by the browser.

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The page, edge buttons and start screen |
| `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js` | Installable PWA and offline cache |
| `js/content.js` | All words, vehicles and voice lines |
| `js/art.js` | Hub, fire engine scene, Pip and stickers |
| `js/audio.js` | Sound effects and the narrator voice |
| `js/app.js` | Router, Pip's outfits, moods and actions, fire engine, settings and shared toolkit |
| `js/worlds.js` | The list of worlds, Pip's map (the story picker) and the place drawings |
| `js/vehicles2.js`, `js/vehicles3.js` | The other eight vehicles |
| `js/match.js`, `js/games.js`, `js/games4.js`, `js/games5.js` | The first ten games |
| `js/ballrun.js` | The ball run engine (physics by Matter.js, in `js/lib/`) and Water Run, with its 12 puzzles |
| `js/train.js` | The syllable train engine and Word Train, with its 12 words |
| `tools/make-voice.mjs` | Makes the narrator clips and the Word Train beat clips with Google Text-to-Speech |
| `js/rewards.js` | Sticker book tabs, a sticker page per world, badges, Word Book and certificate |
| `js/sorter.js` | The sorting engine (drag each item to the right place), used by Who Eats What? and Animal Homes |
| `js/story.js` | The picture story engine (pages read aloud, with tappable pictures) |
| `js/zoo-art.js` | Zoo drawings: 18 animals, food, habitats, Zara the zookeeper and scenery |
| `js/zoo.js` | The zoo world pack: its words, story, Snack Run puzzles, Zoo Train words, sorting rounds, home screen, stickers and badge |
| `js/monster-art.js` | Monster Maths drawings: the six shape monsters (with moods), shapes, numerals and dots, cookies, the monster builder and Monster Town scenery |
| `js/monsters.js` | The Monster Maths world pack: its words, story, Gobble Run puzzles (numbered balls), Monster Train words, Shape Snacks rounds, Feed the Monster and Monster Shapes, home screen, stickers and badge |
| `js/school-art.js` | School drawings: classroom things, children in uniform, Mrs Bell, Nibbles the hamster, crayons, pencils, colour pots and the school bag items |
| `js/school.js` | The School world pack: its words, story, Crayon Run puzzles, School Train words, Pack My Bag rounds, Line Up!, classroom home screen, stickers and badge |
| `fonts/` | Andika and Fredoka, bundled for offline use (SIL Open Font License) |
| `docs/` | Style guide and image prompts |

To run it locally, serve the folder over HTTP (for example with `python3 -m http.server`) and open `http://localhost:8000`. Service workers do not run from `file://`.

**When you change any file,** bump `VERSION` in `sw.js`. Installed tablets then pick up the new version the next time they open the app while online. Add any new file to the `FILES` list in `sw.js` too.

**Recorded voice:** every fixed line has a clip in `audio/voice/`, named by its line id (for example `hub.welcome.mp3`). After adding lines, run `GOOGLE_TTS_API_KEY=... node tools/make-voice.mjs` to make only the missing clips; it also updates `js/voice.js` (the clip list, cached offline by `sw.js`) and bumps `VERSION`. Lines with a `{name}`-style blank stay on the device voice.

**Word Train beats:** each word in `js/train.js` has `beats` (shown on the carriages) and `say` (how the voice is asked to pronounce each beat). The tool has the voice say the word beat by beat with long pauses, then cuts it at the pauses into `syl.<word>.<n>.mp3` (needs ffmpeg). To redo one word after changing its `say` spelling: `node tools/make-voice.mjs --only syl.helmet --force`.

**Ball run puzzles:** each puzzle in `js/ballrun.js` lists the hose column, the fire's column, any bolted-on pieces, the pieces in the tray, and a `hint` (one way to solve it, shown after two misses). `BallRun.Sim.run(puzzle, pieces)` runs the physics without a screen, so a puzzle can be checked as solvable before it ships.

**Adding a world:** in `js/worlds.js`, set `built: true` and a `home` screen name for the world, then add its screens to `App.screens` and list each one in `App.screenWorld` (for example `App.screenWorld.zooGate = 'zoo'`) so Pip wears that world's outfit and the home button goes to that world's home. `js/zoo.js` is the example to copy: it adds games with the shared engines, `BallRun.mount`, `WordTrain.mount`, `Sorter.mount` and `Story.mount`, each given the world's own levels, pictures and lines, with `world` and `back` set so its stickers go on the world's page and "more games" returns to the world's home. A world's sticker page and badge go in `Rewards.pages` and `Rewards.extraBadges`. Pip's outfits are in `PIP_OUTFITS` in `js/art.js`. Pip's moods and actions: `App.Pip.mood('cheer' | 'surprised' | 'thinking')` and `App.Pip.act('jump' | 'dance' | 'wave')`.

## Build history

### Pip's World, Phase 5a: School
- School opens from Pip's map. The classroom home screen has six things to visit (each gives a fact about the school day and a sticker) and five games along the bottom. Pip wears his school cap and bag.
- **First Day**, a picture story read aloud: Pip's first day in Reception.
- **Crayon Run** (ball run): crayons roll from the crayon box into the pot of their colour; from puzzle 3 a pot of another colour sits under the chute. The 12 puzzles are the Gobble Run layouts in a new order, each checked solvable again by running its physics.
- **School Train** (syllables): 12 school words, with 2, 3 or 4 beats.
- **Pack My Bag**, a sorting game: school things into the bag, home things stay at home.
- **Line Up!**, a new size-ordering game: shortest to tallest, shortest to longest, and tallest to shortest.
- 11 school stickers on their own sticker book page, and a Star Pupil badge.
- 162 new voice clips: 118 lines, 12 words and 32 School Train beats.

### Pip's World, Phase 4: Monster Maths
- Monster Maths opens from Pip's map. Monster Town's home screen has six shape monsters on the street (tap one to count its eyes, horns, legs, points or sides, and win its sticker) and six games along the bottom. Pip wears his monster horns.
- **Hungry Monsters**, a picture story read aloud: Pip visits Monster Town, where monsters eat numbers, and helps Gobble add two and three.
- **Gobble Run** (ball run): each ball carries a number (1, 2 or 3). The monster says each number as it eats it, a sum builds up above it (2 + 1 + 3 = 6), and it burps the total. From puzzle 3 a sleeping monster lies under the chute. The 12 puzzles are the Snack Run puzzles mirrored left to right, each checked solvable again by running its physics. The ball run engine now tells a world which ball was eaten, so balls can carry numbers.
- **Monster Train** (syllables): 12 number and shape words, with 2, 3 or 4 beats.
- **Feed the Monster**, a new counting game: count cookies into a monster's mouth (up to 5, then up to 10), then add two plates and pick the total from three numbers. The cookies counted so far line up under the monster, numbered, in each plate's colour.
- **Monster Shapes**, a new building game: pick a body shape, eyes, horns, mouth, legs and colour, and the finished monster goes into My Pictures.
- **Shape Snacks**, a sorting game: each monster eats biscuits of its own shape.
- 12 Monster Maths stickers on their own sticker book page, and a Number Champion badge.
- 206 new voice clips: 174 lines and 32 Monster Train beats.

### Pip's World, Phase 3: the Zoo
- The Zoo opens from Pip's map. Its home screen has six animals behind a fence (tap one for a fact and its sticker) and five games along the bottom. Pip wears his safari hat.
- **Feeding Time**, a picture story read aloud: Pip helps Zara the zookeeper feed the monkeys, penguins, giraffe, elephant and panda.
- **Snack Run** (ball run): food rolls from the keeper's chute to the hungry animal. From puzzle 3, a second animal waits right under the chute, so the food must be steered past it ("Oops! That food is for the giraffe."). Three hearts fill up as the animal eats. All 12 puzzles were checked solvable by running their physics, with the empty board failing and no single piece solving a two-piece puzzle.
- **Zoo Train** (syllables): 12 animal words, with 2, 3 or 4 beats.
- **Who Eats What?** and **Animal Homes**, two sorting games that get a little harder each time.
- 11 zoo stickers on the zoo's own sticker book page, and a Zoo Keeper badge.
- The game engines now take a world pack, so the zoo needed no new ball run or train code. New shared engines for sorting and picture stories are ready for the next worlds.
- 163 new voice clips: 131 lines and 32 Zoo Train beats.

### Pip's World, Phase 1: the shared core
- The app opens on **Pip's map**, a story picker with 7 places joined by a winding path. The Rescue Station opens; the other six show a "Soon!" sign (worlds are never locked), and Pip tries on that world's outfit and says it's coming soon.
- **Pip has outfits** for every world (rescue helmet and hi-vis, safari hat, monster horns, school cap and bag, supermarket apron, party hat, puppy-sitter apron), **moods** (surprised, cheering, thinking) and **actions** (jump, dance, wave). Tap Pip and he jumps.
- An orange map button on the Rescue Station takes children back to the map.
- The **sticker book has a page per world**. The Rescue Station page holds every sticker earned so far; the other pages show empty slots until their world opens.
- Saved stickers, badges, words and pictures carry over unchanged.

### Pip's World, Phase 2: ball run and syllable train, in the Rescue Station
- **Water Run** (ball run): a pegboard with a fire hose at the top and a fire at the bottom. Children drag pieces from the tray (ramp, long ramp, curve, funnel, spring, fan, see-saw and bucket), tap a piece to turn it around, and blow Pip's whistle. Water drops roll through the run, and Pip counts each drop that lands on the fire; three put it out. **Build** is free play with every piece. **Puzzles** 1 to 12 get harder: one piece, then two, then a see-saw or bucket. Bolted-on bricks appear in the last one. After two misses Pip makes a piece's spot glow. Each puzzle was checked solvable by running its physics.
- **Word Train** (syllables): **Clap it**: Pip says a word and its beats, then the child taps the drum once per beat and the carriages light up. **Build it**: the carriages are muddled; tap one to hear it, drag them onto the train in order. **Swap it**: one carriage is silly ("hy-lance!"); drag the right one onto it. Each carriage shows its beat number as dots and its colour, with the beat written above for early readers. 12 rescue words with 2, 3 or 4 beats.
- Both games earn a sticker (21 in all), and the Word Train stages and solved puzzles get a star.
- New narrator clips for every new line, plus a clip for each of the 30 beats, cut from the same voice.
